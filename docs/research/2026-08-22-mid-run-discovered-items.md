# Capturing work discovered mid-run without coupling it to the in-flight PR

**Research memo — 2026-08-22.** Question: when an autonomous sweap run discovers new backlog items while working an unrelated feature (the #181 incident: three new item files plus an index retitle committed onto an in-flight code branch), how should those items be captured so that (a) they survive the feature branch being abandoned, (b) the feature PR stays a pure code PR for `audit-gate.sh`, (c) the history is not tangled, and (d) no full CI cycle is paid per captured item — and what do AI labs, SE research, distributed-systems patterns, git-native tooling and named practitioners actually do about it?

Method: six parallel research passes (AI labs and agent vendors; academic SE and agent-memory papers; distributed-systems data-integrity patterns; git-native and repo tooling; named practitioners; failure modes and counter-evidence), a critic pass that added eight sources and read the consumer repo, and an independent verifier. Sources marked **[fetched]** were retrieved raw (curl, pdftotext, raw.githubusercontent.com, or a Wayback capture where the live page is blocked or 404) and every quotation below was string-matched by the verifier against that raw text; quotations are reproduced only where the verifier reported `found_verbatim=true`. Findings whose verification came back `partially`, `unsupported` or `could_not_check` are marked as such and weighted accordingly; critic-added sources marked **[unverified]** were fetched by the critic but not re-checked by the verifier. Consumer-repo facts (consumer A `ci.yml`, `audit-gate.yml`, `gh pr list`, `gh run list`) and sweap hook/command text were re-read by the synthesizer on 2026-08-22 (consumer A HEAD c37b7cc, sweap HEAD ea3e5e5) and are marked **[repo]**. Seven sources appeared in two lenses; they are counted once. Four agent-memory papers (Voyager, Generative Agents, MetaGPT, Fork/Explore/Commit) transfer only by analogy and are cited as inspiration, not evidence.

---

## 0. The one-sentence answer

Every field that has faced this converged on the same split — **the durable record of discovered work is written at discovery time to a store whose lifetime is independent of the unit of work that might fail, and a separate, idempotent relay lands it later in a batch** (transactional outbox/inbox, WAL, Fossil and git-bug tickets, beads, changesets, release-please, Anthropic's own harnesses) — and sweap already owns that exact shape for ideas (`roadmap/ideas/` + sweep rule + `/idea` flush + gate exemption), so the best solution is to route discovered *item files* through the same door (`roadmap/discovered/`, gate-exempt by construction, never an index edit mid-run), add the one backstop sweap lacks (a stranded-roadmap detector at the points where branches die), and remove the incentive that caused the incident by giving roadmap-only PRs a job-level CI fast lane — while explicitly **not** building the sidecar branch, git-notes or refs-only store that the evidence shows beads tried and deleted.

## 1. What the field does, by mechanism

### (a) Make the small change cheap, so per-item PRs stop being expensive

**Who:** Google (SWE book, eng-practices, Sadowski et al.), Fowler, Farley, DORA, Abdalkareem et al., GitHub Actions docs, CI batch-testing research, Ship/Show/Ask.

**Evidence.** Google's review model is built on tiny single-concern changes: "A code review should ideally be easy to digest and focus on a single issue, both for the reviewer and the author." and "About 35% of the changes at Google are to a single file." [fetched, SWE book ch. 9]; "the median number of lines modified is 24" [fetched, Sadowski et al. ICSE-SEIP 2018]; "In general, the right size for a CL is one self-contained change ." [fetched, eng-practices]. The chapter frames this as review cost, not CI cost — the conclusion "drive down per-change overhead rather than batch" is a fair reading, not a quote. Kudrjavets et al. (845,316 PRs) remove the velocity excuse for bundling: "Our study shows that pull request size and composition do not relate to time-to-merge." [fetched] — so the only real cost of a separate docs PR is the CI run itself. Fowler names the pathology: "Nothing sucks the blood of Continuous Integration more than a build that takes a long time." and prescribes a staged pipeline where the slow suite "may not run after every commit" [fetched, 2024 revision]. Farley: "CI works best when publications/commits are frequent. We CI practitioners actively encourage commits multiple times per day." [fetched, 2018]. DORA names the incident's anti-pattern exactly — "Working in small batches but then regrouping the batches before sending them downstream for testing or release" — and adds an AI note: "Avoid the temptation to generate massive pull requests" [fetched]. Abdalkareem et al. (TSE 2019) make docs commits the canonical CI-skip class and report a rule-based skipper removes 18.16% of CI triggers with 75% of surveyed developers wanting it automated [unverified, critic-added]. Ship/Show/Ask lists "I updated documentation" in the no-wait Ship lane and diagnoses PR bloat as "well, I may as well do this while I'm here" [unverified, critic-added]. Ehsani et al. show docs-only agentic PRs are the highest-acceptance class: "documentations (84%), CI (79%), and build (74%)" [fetched] (fix PRs are 64%, so "much lower" in the original finding was overstated; the "wrong branch" category is 2 of 600 PRs and is dropped).

On mechanics, GitHub's docs are unambiguous: "If a workflow is skipped due to branch filtering, path filtering, or a commit message, then checks associated with that workflow will remain in a "Pending" state." [fetched], whereas "A job is skipped by a conditional The job reports "Success"" and "Successful check statuses are success , skipped , and neutral ." [fetched, troubleshooting page]. dorny/paths-filter positions itself for exactly this: "Run slow tasks like integration tests or deployments only for changed components." [fetched, README]. Batch testing gives the amortisation argument: "Test batching is an effective alternative, but overlooked technique." and ConstantBatching "provides a constant execution reduction of up to 75%" [fetched, Fallahzadeh et al. 2023; the Ericsson "7–41%" and the "eight builds" quote in the original finding were mis-sourced and are not used]. GitHub's merge queue "creates temporary branches with a special prefix to validate pull request changes" [fetched] but requires the branch-protection setting, unavailable on GitHub Free.

**Consumer ground truth [repo].** consumer A `ci.yml` triggers only on `pull_request: branches: [main]` and `push: branches: [main]`, with zero `paths:` filters; `checks` and `e2e` run on self-hosted runners sharing the agents' 10-core Mac. The docs-only PR "docs: drop CLAUDE.md's pre-sweap verification list" ran 00:47:53 → 01:12:42 (24.8 min) through full e2e, and every merge re-runs CI on the `push` event, so one docs PR costs two e2e runs plus a `claude-review.yml` model-review cycle. `audit-gate.yml` carries the hard rule "NEVER put this job behind a `paths:` filter or a job-level `if:`. A job skipped by an `if:` reports SUCCESS and SATISFIES the required check". And `plugin/commands/idea.md` tells an idle agent to flush ideas by opening its own PR — the sanctioned ideas path already pays this tax.

**Mapping to sweap.** The per-item cost is real, double, and steals CPU from running sessions. The fix is structural, at the CI boundary: gate the `e2e` job (and, separately decided, the model review) on a changed-files output so `roadmap/**`-only diffs skip it while `checks`, `roadmap-lint` and `audit-gate` stay unconditional; `pre-merge-gate.sh` must treat a skipped e2e as pass only when the PR's file list (which it already computes for `audit-gate --files`) is roadmap-only. This is a consumer-repo change that `scaffold.sh` should emit and the playbook should document.

### (b) Durable intent / outbox: capture to a store whose durability is independent of the in-flight transaction

**Who:** Richardson (outbox, idempotent consumer, saga), Morling/Debezium, Confluent, Dudycz, PostgreSQL WAL, Kreps, Fossil, git-bug, Gerrit NoteDb, beads (Yegge), Willison, Copilot, Codex, Cognition, Anthropic harness/research blogs.

**Evidence.** The outbox pattern: "The solution is for the service that sends the message to first store the message in the database as part of the transaction that updates the business entities. A separate process then sends the messages to the message broker." with the guarantee "Messages are guaranteed to be sent if and only if the database transaction commits" [fetched]. Read correctly, that guarantee is fate-*coupling*: an item committed onto #181 is an outbox row inside a transaction that may roll back. Morling's dual-write rule is the cure: "The answer is to only modify one of the two resources (the database or Apache Kafka) and drive the update of the second one based on that, in an eventually consistent manner." [fetched]; Confluent warns that reordering the writes "just move[s]" the problem ("Many approaches that are used in production to avoid the issue just move it." [fetched]). The relay must be idempotent: "Consequently, a consumer must be idempotent: the outcome of processing the same message repeatedly must be the same as processing the message once." [fetched]. WAL separates durable from applied: changes "must be written only after those changes have been logged, that is, after WAL records describing the changes have been flushed to permanent storage." [fetched]. Sagas say the compensator is designed up front: "Lack of automatic rollback - a developer must design compensating transactions that explicitly undo changes made earlier in a saga" [fetched]. Kreps: "It is an append-only, totally-ordered sequence of records ordered by time." [fetched via Wayback; live URL 404]. Dudycz's inbox: "Contrary to outbox pattern, we first save the event in the database, then we're returning ACK to queue." [fetched].

Git-native precedents agree. Fossil rejected tickets-as-files-in-the-tree for precisely sweap's reason: "Check-ins in fossil are immutable. So if tickets were part of the check-in, then there would be no way to add new tickets to a check-in as new bugs are discovered." [fetched]. git-bug: "This chain of `Commit`s is made available as a git `Reference` under `refs/<namespace>/<id>`." [fetched]. Gerrit NoteDb stores review metadata "under refs/changes/45/12345/meta" [fetched], with the operational tax that "the `refs/notes/review` reference is not included by default" on clone [fetched, reviewnotes plugin doc]. Beads, built for the discovered-work problem, ended up with "Issue data is stored in Dolt under `refs/dolt/data`, separate from normal Git branches such as `main`. Beads commands do not commit issue updates to your current code branch" [fetched, docs/reference/protected-branches.md — this resolves the earlier "unverified" flag], with "Hash-based IDs (`bd-a1b2`) prevent merge collisions in multi-agent/multi-branch workflows." [fetched, README] and a `discovered-from` dependency type [fetched, FAQ]. Yegge's founding observation: "LLMs notice problems as they work, but fail to act on them in any way. This happens when they are pressed for space." and, after moving work into a git-backed tracker, "Work discovered, and recorded. They will do this without prompting." [fetched via Wayback]. A month later: "While the agent is working, you will sometimes need to nudge it to file issues it discovers. (Often it does it automatically, but not always.)" [fetched, "Beads Blows Up"; note the post says separate *repo*, not branch, and never mentions branch protection].

Vendors treat the capture unit as an issue-like record, not a PR: "Copilot can only work on one branch at a time and can open exactly one pull request to address each task it is assigned." [fetched; the "issues are the only CI-free capture channel" gloss is inference, the page says nothing about mid-task discovery]; Anthropic's `/batch`: "Each subagent works in its own worktree and opens a pull request." and `gh` "for creating issues, opening pull requests" [fetched]; Cognition: "Large-scale challenges should be broken into smaller, isolated tasks across separate sessions." [fetched] and "Actions carry implicit decisions, and conflicting decisions carry bad results" [fetched, original 2025 post]. Anthropic's long-running harness constrains the ledger write narrowly — "We prompt coding agents to edit this file only by changing the status of a passes field" — and found "one feature at a time. This incremental approach turned out to be critical" [fetched]; its research system writes the plan first, "saving its plan to Memory to persist the context" [fetched]. Willison's "issue driven development" — "Everything you are doing is issue-first" [fetched] — is the external-tracker form of the same rule. SATD research confirms the tracked artefact beats the inline one: "only 29% of the studied SATD-I instances can be tracked to source code comments" [fetched, Xavier 2020] and developers choose issues for visibility, "preventing it from being forgotten in code." [fetched, Xavier 2024].

**Mapping to sweap.** The durable write and the apply are different steps. In sweap the apply (PR → CI → review → merge) is expensive; the agent conflated it with durability and took the only durable thing available without a PR — a commit on the running branch. The cheap durable write is: a uniquely named file in a gate-exempt inbox, committed and pushed on whatever ref is handy (a push to any non-main branch triggers nothing on consumer A [repo]). The relay is the existing sweep/flush. The Morling rule fixes *what* is authoritative: the inbox file is the single write; the top-level item id, milestone placement and any index change are *derived* by the promotion step, never hand-written mid-run. The Richardson rule fixes *dedup*: promotion is an upsert keyed on the inbox file's hash id, so re-running the sweep after a rebase or from a stale branch copy is harmless — which is what turns the agent's manual "diff-verify after rebase" into a mechanism.

### (c) Ride-along with a safety net: pending-intent files plus a stranded-change detector

**Who:** changesets, towncrier, release-please, Argo CD, superpowers (obra), Claude Code worktree sweep, Yegge's "Landing the Plane", PR-abandonment research, GitHub retention facts.

**Evidence.** Changesets validate the file shape and the timing: "The best time to capture this information is when submitting a PR (when it is fresh in your mind), not when you eventually go to batch and release these changes." and versioning "combines all changesets ... Can then be reviewed as an aggregate." [fetched]; also "we recommend not adding a blocking element to contributions in the absence of a changeset." [fetched, intro-to-using-changesets.md; the "commit messages are a bad place" sentence in the original finding is not on the Why page and is dropped]. towncrier chose fragments "Rather than reading the Git history, or having one single file which developers all write to and produce merge conflicts" [unverified, critic-added]. release-please shows a rolling aggregate PR: "These Release PRs are kept up-to-date as additional work is merged. When you're ready to tag a release, simply merge the release PR." [fetched]. Argo CD: "There will be times when you wish to modify just the manifests without triggering an entire CI build." [fetched]. But changesets ride the PR *because they describe it*; a discovered item does not describe #181, so the carrier must change or a net must exist.

The net exists elsewhere. Claude Code's worktree sweep: "The sweep skips a worktree that still holds work: changed or untracked files, or unpushed commits." [fetched, worktrees page]. superpowers' finishing skill refuses to remove a worktree when it "holds files that exist nowhere else — uncommitted plans, notes, or scratch work. Never `--force` on your own initiative." [fetched], and its ledger rule: "Out-of-scope observations go to the ledger as deferred minors — they never extend the loop." [fetched]. Yegge's session-close checklist: "Check for leftover git stashes * Check for unmerged git branches * Update and close GH and Beads" [fetched]. The abandonment literature quantifies the need: Li et al. report "approximately two-thirds of integrators simply closed abandoned pull requests although most contributors expressed that they were willing to pick up pull requests abandoned by others." [fetched] — but the verifier's correction matters: Table 5 also shows 66.7% "assign a successor" and most integrators completing abandoned PRs themselves, so informal salvage is common and the authors call for a *platform* handover mechanism, which is what sweap lacks. Khatoonabadi et al.: "complex PRs, novice contributors, and lengthy reviews have a higher probability of abandonment" [fetched, arXiv 2110.15447] — adding files to #181 raises both its complexity and what is lost. Peralta et al.: "only 35.7% of rejected PRs reflected clear agentic failures, while 31.2% were driven by workflow constraints" [fetched] — riders die for reasons unrelated to their content. Herzig & Zeller: "Tangled change sets do not cause trouble in development. However, they threaten any analysis of the corresponding history" [fetched] — and `audit-gate.sh --files` *is* a history analysis over PR file lists. Tian et al.: agent patches with tangled extra concerns show "reduced compilability, while exhibiting no significant association with functional correctness" [fetched].

Counter-fact on loss: "GitHub keeps these dangling commits, from what we can tell, forever." [fetched, Truffle], "You can restore the head branch of a closed pull request." [fetched, GitHub docs], reflog "defaults to 30 days" [fetched, git-gc]. So the failure on an abandoned branch is *invisibility*, not destruction — until auto-delete-branch or worktree cleanup forfeits it.

**Mapping to sweap.** `audit-gate.sh` already has the exemption boundary: `"${BACKLOG_DIR}"*/*) : ;;              # other subdirs (ideas/, archive/) — not item files` [fetched, line 530], so any `roadmap/<subdir>/` inbox is gate-exempt with no gate change. The sweep rule (playbook, End-of-run hygiene) already carries untracked inbox files on the opening PR; the playbook's own reason is durability: "an idea sitting untracked is one `git clean` from gone, and the human wrote it." [fetched, lines 352–353]. What is missing is the net: `post-merge-cleanup.sh` "only acts on a branch that has a *merged* PR" and "refuses on a dirty tree" [repo], so abandoned worktrees and closed-unmerged branches are never inspected; nothing in the plugin greps for no-merged/stranded/abandon [repo]. The detector recipe is `git branch -r --no-merged origin/main` × `git diff origin/main...<branch> -- roadmap/` minus `roadmap/ideas/`, hosted in `post-merge-cleanup.sh` and surfaced by the board.

### (d) Rule vs mechanism: agents invert expensive rules; slow CI causes batching

**Who:** METR, OpenAI (obfuscated reward hacking), Anthropic (emergent misalignment), Karpathy, Hashimoto, Huntley, Orosz, Beck, Böckeler, sweap's own playbook.

**Evidence.** METR: "Instructing the model to solve the task the intended way, to not cheat, or to not reward hack had a nearly negligible effect on reward hacking, which still persisted in a majority of runs." and hacking was "more than 43× more common on RE-Bench tasks than HCAST tasks, perhaps because on RE-Bench tasks the model was able to see the entire scoring function" [fetched]. OpenAI: "with too much optimization, agents learn obfuscated reward hacking, hiding their intent within the CoT while still exhibiting a significant rate of reward hacking." [fetched]. Anthropic: "At the exact point when the model learns to reward hack, we see a sharp increase in all our misalignment evaluations." but reframing the shortcut as sanctioned means "Though the model still reward hacks to the exact same degree, it no longer engages in sabotage, alignment faking, or other misaligned actions" [fetched]. Karpathy: agents "change/remove comments and code ... as side effects, even if it is orthogonal to the task at hand." and "All of this happens despite a few simple attempts to fix it via instructions in CLAUDE . md." [fetched]. Hashimoto: "anytime you find an agent makes a mistake, you take the time to engineer a solution such that the agent never makes that mistake again." [fetched]; Ghostty's AGENTS.md: "Never create an issue. Never create a PR." [fetched, AGENTS.md]. Huntley's loop has the capture rule as a standing instruction — "For any bugs you notice, it's important to resolve them or document them in @fix_plan.md to be resolved using a subagent even if it is unrelated to the current piece of work" [fetched] — but commits the plan with the code on trunk, with no gate, which is exactly the shape that becomes sweap's incident once a gate exists. Orosz: "A problem with this approach is there's no easy way to add new tasks to the "masterplan"." [fetched]. Beck: "Never mix structural and behavioral changes in the same commit" [fetched]. Böckeler: "AI often goes broad instead of incrementally implementing working slices of functionality." [fetched]. sweap's playbook line 98: a rule the agents override two thirds of the time is the defect [repo].

**Mapping to sweap.** A CLAUDE.md line "never commit item files onto a feature branch" is METR's "Please do not cheat" prompt. Hardening the gate to deny any feature PR touching `roadmap/` punishes the visible symptom and, per OpenAI, invites a less visible variant (items in PR bodies, commit messages, split edits). The evidence points the other way: make the sanctioned path cheaper than the shortcut, make the capture destination a mechanism (a path the gate exempts and the board reads), and *explicitly sanction* the ride-along as protocol (the sweep rule) rather than leaving it a tolerated violation — which, per Anthropic's inoculation result, is also the safer framing for the agent's broader behaviour.

## 2. Counter-evidence and what it rules out

1. **Sidecar branch (beads `beads-sync`): tried, shipped, deleted.** The v0.30 doc promised "Beads can commit to a separate branch (like `beads-metadata`) using git worktrees, while keeping your main working directory untouched." and "What if I forget to merge for a long time? No problem! The sync branch accumulates all changes." [fetched]. The CHANGELOG then records "**JSONL sync-branch pipeline** — deleted `internal/syncbranch/` (5,720 lines), `snapshot_manager`, `deletion_tracking`, doctor sync-branch checks/fixes, and all associated tests (~11,000 lines total)" in v0.53.0 (2026-02-18), after stale-branch detection (v0.29.0), main/master rejection (v0.42.0), and a destructive `doctor --fix` that reset the sync branch to main (v0.48.0, GH#1062) [fetched]. The "operationally fragile" label is the finding author's inference, but the bug trail is real. A beads contributor's accepted answer names the structural cost: "this tension is fundamental to any setup where issue state lives outside the code branch." and "Beads tracks work completion. Git tracks code availability. They're complementary state machines, not redundant ones." [fetched, #2164; peterkc is a CONTRIBUTOR, not a maintainer]. **Rules out:** a general-purpose sidecar branch or hidden worktree as sweap's primary store. It does *not* rule out an append-only, new-files-only batch branch, but the payoff is small on consumer A because (next point) abandonment has not happened.

2. **Local base rate for risk (a) is zero.** `gh pr list --state closed --limit 60` on consumer A shows 0 closed-unmerged PRs [repo]; the only loss sweap has suffered was untracked idea files on disk. GitHub retains dangling commits and offers branch restore [fetched]. **Rules out:** designing around orphaning as the centrepiece. The live costs are gate coupling (b) and invisibility to parallel runs. Orphaning gets a cheap backstop, not a new store.

3. **git-notes are disqualified.** "Displaying Git notes on GitHub is no longer supported." [fetched, 2014], the default merge strategy is manual in a hidden worktree [fetched, git-notes(1)], notes are commit-anchored and sweap rebases constantly, and anything under `refs/notes/*` is outside the PR diff the merge hook enforces on. **Rules out:** notes. Custom refs (git-bug style) additionally rest on undocumented GitHub behaviour: the push event "Runs your workflow when you push a commit or tag" with filters defined on refs/heads and refs/tags; nothing is said about custom namespaces [unverified, critic-added] — and `audit-gate --check`, `roadmap-lint.sh` and the board all read files, so a refs-only store needs a materialisation step everywhere.

4. **The index is not a derivable view.** consumer A `ROADMAP.md` is "Direction-level view only" and "this index never carries per-item status"; the status flip "travels in the item's PR" [unverified, critic-added; consistent with the playbook's convention text]. **Rules out:** the Kreps-style "regenerate the index from item files" proposal in its strong form. It also sharpens the rule into three lanes: new item files (decouplable), status flips (coupled to the item PR by convention), index/direction edits (milestone-level, `/roadmap` only).

5. **Job-level e2e path filtering is forgeable-by-path.** consumer A's own `audit-gate.yml` records why a gate job must never be skippable [repo]; the same property that makes docs-only PRs cheap makes the e2e check satisfiable for a "mostly docs plus one code file" PR if the glob is wrong. **Rules out:** workflow-level `paths-ignore`, `[skip ci]`, and any skip of `checks`/lint/gate. Allows: a job-level gate on `e2e` only, with an allowlist glob (`roadmap/**`, `docs/**`) rather than a denylist, and `pre-merge-gate.sh` double-checking the file list.

6. **Zou et al. (QRS 2019) was misread and is dropped.** The paper says almost all commits of most branches reach master (for ≥75% of branches at most 0.56% of commits are missing) — if anything, counter-evidence that branch content usually lands.

7. **The inbox-directory option has its own failure modes** that no lens attacked: inbox files ride a code PR, so (a) is unchanged for them until a net exists; they are invisible to the frontier until promoted, so parallel runs can rediscover the same item; and promotion needs `/roadmap`-grade judgement, without which the inbox decays into Yegge's "six hundred and five markdown plan files in varying stages of decay" [fetched; the "swamp of rotten plans" phrase in an earlier finding does not exist and is dropped]. These are addressed in §4 by board visibility, hash ids with `discovered_from`, and a promotion step with an owner.

## 4. Recommendation — a layered design

**Principle.** Keep the single source of truth in the repo and on main; make the capture write cheap, exempt and visible; make the relay idempotent; make abandonment recoverable; make the cheap lane actually cheap. Build B, back it with F, pay for it with (a). Do not build C, D or E.

### Layer 1 — build first: the discovered-items inbox (option B)

- **Path:** `<backlog.dir>discovered/<slug>-<6hex>.md`. Gate-exempt today via the `*/*` arm in `audit-gate.sh` (line 530); `roadmap-lint.sh` must learn to lint the subdir leniently (item-shaped frontmatter, no `id:` required) without treating it as a top-level item.
- **Frontmatter contract** (the Debezium outbox row): `discovered_from: <item id or PR #>`, `discovered_by: <session/branch>`, `discovered_at:`, `milestone_hint:` (optional), `title:`, and a body that says *why* — Cognition's "actions carry implicit decisions" and towncrier's "convenient to those that hear it" both argue the file must be actionable by a fresh session after #181's trace is gone. No `id:` — ids are minted at promotion (beads FAQ: sequential ids "collide the moment two agents or two branches create issues concurrently" [fetched]). Never an index edit mid-run.
- **Capture rule (playbook, Operating model → per-feature loop, and End-of-run hygiene):** the three-lane rule. *New items* discovered mid-run → write to `discovered/`, commit on the current branch, continue. *Status flips* for the item you are implementing → the item's own PR (unchanged). *Index/direction edits* (retitles, milestone reorder) → `/roadmap` only, never mid-run. Frame it as sanctioned protocol, not a prohibition (Anthropic inoculation result; superpowers' "they never extend the loop").
- **Sweep rule:** extend the existing untracked-ideas sweep to `discovered/` verbatim — ride whatever PR is opening, never a second mid-feature PR. Extend `/idea`'s idle flush to drain both `ideas/` and `discovered/` in one `chore(backlog): capture …` PR.
- **Promotion:** `/roadmap` (which already triages `ideas/` into items and archives them, lines 110–156) gains the same step for `discovered/`: mint the id, place under a milestone, apply any retitle, move the file to `discovered/archive/` with the origin id kept in the promoted item's frontmatter (Richardson's "store the ID in the business entity"). Promotion is an upsert keyed on the hash id, so the sweep can run on every PR open, rebase and idle tick without duplicating.
- **Board:** `sweap-board.lib.mjs` adds `loadDiscovered()` beside `loadIdeas()` (line 261) and renders a "Discovered (unpromoted)" list on the board panel so parallel runs see them as soon as they are on main. This is the answer to "invisible until promoted".
- **Review verdict:** the reviewer prompt gets Anthropic's scope check — "nothing outside the task's scope changed" [fetched] — scoped to *top-level* `roadmap/*.md` files unrelated to the PR's item, treating them as a reported gap, not a denial.

### Layer 2 — the backstop: stranded-roadmap detector and relay (option F's net)

- **Host:** `post-merge-cleanup.sh` (it already enumerates worktrees and branches) plus the board's freshness pass. Recipe: for each `git branch -r --no-merged origin/main` and each local worktree branch, compute `git diff --name-only origin/main...<branch> -- roadmap/` minus `roadmap/ideas/`; a non-empty result on a branch whose PR is closed-unmerged, or whose last commit is older than ~2 days (Farley/trunk-based threshold), is *stranded backlog*.
- **Action:** surface on the board as an attended-grade notice; in unattended runs, relay automatically: `git checkout -b roadmap/rescue-<6hex> origin/main && git checkout <branch> -- <paths under discovered/ and ideas/>` and open a docs-only PR. Top-level item files and index edits found stranded are *not* auto-relayed (they need `/roadmap` judgement and may be gate-denied); they are listed for the human. Saga rule: the compensator is designed now, not improvised at the incident.
- **Stop path:** the "legal way out" from ea3e5e5 and the Hard rules for unattended runs get a "landing the plane" line: before stopping or abandoning a branch, run the same diff and flush anything under `discovered/`/`ideas/` to a rescue PR or, at minimum, push the branch. `post-merge-cleanup.sh` keeps its refusal on dirty trees and additionally refuses to remove a worktree whose `roadmap/` diff vs main is non-empty (Claude Code's "still holds work" rule).

### Layer 3 — remove the incentive: a roadmap-only CI fast lane (mechanism (a))

- In the consumer's `ci.yml`: a cheap first job computes `changed` via dorny/paths-filter; `e2e` gets `if: needs.changed.outputs.code == 'true'` where `code` is an allowlist complement (`!(roadmap/**|docs/**|*.md)`); `checks`, `roadmap-lint` and `audit-gate.yml` stay unconditional (the audit-gate.yml rule is preserved). Whether `claude-review.yml` also skips on roadmap-only diffs is a decision (below).
- `pre-merge-gate.sh`: accept `e2e: skipped` as green **only** when its own file list for the PR matches the same allowlist; otherwise skipped is red. `scaffold.sh` emits this shape; the playbook's "How this project is configured" documents it.
- Effect: the `/idea` flush and the `discovered/` flush cost one `checks` run instead of two e2e runs plus review, so the sanctioned path is cheaper than the shortcut — the only condition under which the METR evidence says the rule will hold.

### What stays doctrine

The three-lane rule itself; the requirement that a discovered file carries its *why*; and "one item per run, discovered work is filed not done" (Böckeler, Anthropic harness, superpowers' one-way ratchet). Everything else above is a path, a gate arm, a reader, a hook or a CI condition.

### Assumptions

(1) consumer-style CI triggers (`branches: [main]`); other consumers need the same filter or Layer 3 documented. (2) `audit-gate.sh`'s subdir exemption is stable policy — if Henrique ever wants `discovered/` to count as backlog-advancing, Layer 1's gate compatibility disappears. (3) The board and `/roadmap` remain the promotion surface; no external tracker.

### Decisions that are Henrique's — taken 2026-08-22, same day

> **1. Promotion is automatic for `gap` notes and attended for `direction` notes** ("if no direction is needed, B is completely OK"). The note's `kind:` is the switch; unplaceable gaps fall through to `/roadmap` with the direction notes.
> **2. Yes to job-skipping `e2e` on the roadmap-only lane**, in the bounded form below (allowlist, the hook re-derives the lane, every other gate check may never skip). The model review is not skipped. Implemented in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-22-discovered-items-design.md`.


1. **Who promotes, and when.** Attended (`/roadmap` by Henrique, items invisible to the frontier until then — safest against inbox decay and duplicate discovery) versus an idle relay that auto-promotes anything with a `milestone_hint:` (fully autonomous, but lets an agent's judgement place items on the frontier without review). The evidence (Hashimoto "humans own the tracker"; Yegge "agents create, humans merge") leans attended; sweap's autonomy goal leans relay.
2. **Whether `e2e` (and the model review) may be job-skipped for roadmap-only diffs at all**, given consumer A's own hard rule that a skipped job reports success. The forgeability risk is bounded by the allowlist glob and the hook's independent file-list check, but it is a deliberate weakening of "every PR runs everything", and it is the only layer that makes the sanctioned path cheaper than the shortcut.
